## Why this section exists

Interviewers have heard “Kafka + Redis + microservices” so often that the phrase itself is a smell. This article catalogs failure patterns and the better framing.

## Dangerous patterns

### 1. Redis as primary database without justification

**Why dangerous:** durability and durability semantics are easy to hand-wave.  
**Better:** Redis accelerates; a system of record is explicit.

### 2. Kafka for synchronous request/response

**Why dangerous:** you reinvent RPC with worse latency and harder debugging.  
**Better:** Kafka for async fan-out, decoupling, replay.

### 3. Sharding on day one

**Why dangerous:** cross-shard complexity before proven bottleneck.  
**Better:** measure the first ceiling; shard when forced.

### 4. Microservices for a whiteboard MVP

**Why dangerous:** distributed monolith of network calls.  
**Better:** modular monolith first unless team/scale demands split.

### 5. Distributed locks everywhere

**Why dangerous:** locks fail in messy ways (expiry, partitions).  
**Better:** ownership via partitions, DB constraints, idempotency keys.

### 6. Eventual consistency for bank balances

**Why dangerous:** customer-visible money bugs.  
**Better:** strong transactional ledger updates; async only for non-ledger side effects.

### 7. Retry without backoff/jitter

**Why dangerous:** retry storms amplify outages.  
**Better:** bounded retries, jitter, circuit breakers.

### 8. No idempotency on payment-like APIs

**Why dangerous:** double charges on timeouts.  
**Better:** idempotency keys + durable dedupe.

### 9. No timeouts

**Why dangerous:** thread/connection exhaustion.  
**Better:** budgets per hop.

### 10. No DLQ / poison handling

**Why dangerous:** one bad message blocks a partition consumer forever.  
**Better:** retry → DLQ → alert → replay tooling.

### 11. Cache without invalidation story

**Why dangerous:** silent staleness or thundering herds.  
**Better:** TTL + version + single-flight.

### 12. Wrong Kafka key

**Why dangerous:** broken ordering or hot partitions.  
**Better:** key = ordering entity (user/order), watch cardinality.

### 13. Hot Redis key / hot DB shard

**Why dangerous:** one node melts while cluster is “fine.”  
**Better:** local cache, split keys, coalescing.

### 14. Sync chain of many services

**Why dangerous:** p99 is the product of failures.  
**Better:** shorten critical path; async the rest.

### 15. No capacity planning

**Why dangerous:** architecture cosplay.  
**Better:** rough QPS/storage before drawing boxes.

## Interview recovery line

If you catch yourself mid-anti-pattern:

> “That would be premature. Let’s keep a simpler design until we identify the bottleneck.”

That sentence is Staff-shaped.

## Remember This
