## The interview problem

**Interviewer:** Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision.

A weak answer narrates office politics. A strong answer shows **judgment, influence without authority, and ownership of outcomes**. Behavioral rounds at senior levels are not soft fillers — they test whether your system-design maturity shows up in people and delivery.

## What interviewers evaluate

| Signal | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| Ownership | You stayed with the problem until production was healthy |
| Judgment | You chose trade-offs deliberately under incomplete data |
| Influence | You changed minds with evidence, not title |
| Communication | You made complexity clear for engineers and stakeholders |
| Learning | You changed behavior after a miss |

## STAR, used like a senior engineer

```text
Situation  → context and stakes (business + technical)
Task       → your responsibility (not the team's vague goal)
Action     → what YOU did, decisions, trade-offs
Result     → measurable outcome + what you would repeat/change
```

Keep Situation short. Spend most time on Action and Result.

## A reusable answer skeleton

1. One-sentence stakes.
2. Constraint that made the problem hard.
3. Options you considered.
4. Decision and why.
5. How you aligned people.
6. Outcome with numbers if possible.
7. Retrospective learning.

## Example prompt map

| Prompt | Focus |
|---|---|
| Conflict with peer | Evidence, respect, escalation path |
| Production incident | Detection, mitigation, prevention |
| Missed deadline | Scope control, stakeholder honesty |
| Mentoring | Specific coaching loop and growth |
| Ambiguity | How you created clarity |

## Common mistakes

- Saying “we” so much that your role disappears
- Blaming other teams
- No measurable result
- No learning
- Over-polished stories that avoid tension

## Practice drill

Prepare **eight stories** covering: conflict, failure, leadership, mentorship, ambiguity, delivery under pressure, customer impact, and a decision you reversed.

For each story, write a 90-second version and a 4-minute version.
