## Why this prompt appears

Staff interviews probe whether you can be trusted with production. The story is less about heroics and more about **orderly mitigation, communication, and prevention**.

## Structure a strong incident story

```text
Detect → Mitigate user impact → Communicate → Diagnose → Fix → Prevent
```

### Detect

How did you know? Alert, customer report, dashboards, canary.

### Mitigate first

Rollback, feature flag, shed load, failover. Interviewers want impact reduction before root-cause perfection.

### Communicate

Who you updated, cadence, and clarity. Executives need impact and ETA; engineers need hypotheses.

### Diagnose and fix

Evidence, not guessing. What logs/metrics/traces changed your mind.

### Prevent

Runbook, test, alert, capacity, design change. A story without prevention sounds incomplete.

## Sample framing

> “P99 checkout latency spiked after a deploy. I rolled back within eight minutes, posted a status update, then used traces to isolate a lock contention path. We added a load test and a canary gate so the same class of change cannot ship dark.”

## Signals that raise seniority

- You protect customers before ego
- You coordinate across teams without drama
- You quantify blast radius
- You leave the system safer than you found it

## Weak patterns

- “I restarted the pod and it worked”
- No stakeholder updates
- Blame without systems thinking
- No follow-up work
