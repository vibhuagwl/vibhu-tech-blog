## Principle

Leaders listen attentively, speak candidly, and treat others respectfully. They are vocally self-critical, even when doing so is awkward or embarrassing. Leaders do not believe their or their team’s body odor smells of perfume. They benchmark themselves and their teams against the best.

## Primary STAR — Deloitte tax integration trust

**Situation:** Multi-party delivery (engineering, tax partner responses, equity admins, UAT). Missed expectations would burn trust quickly.

**Task:** Lead communication and technical delivery so stakeholders trusted status and quality.

**Action:**
1. Candid risk logs (latency of tax responses, error handling gaps) early.
2. Shared demos of async SQS path and failure cases — not only happy path.
3. Invited critique in architecture forums; documented decisions.
4. Owned misses in test data assumptions without blaming QA/partner.
5. Built Playwright regression to make trust evidence-based at release time.

**Result:** Production deployment with regulatory compliance needs met; admins gained tax visibility they could rely on.

## Backup — Incident candor

During Kafka DLQ issues, published clear impact/ETA, admitted recovery tooling gap, then delivered the Replay Tool — trust from honesty + follow-through.

## Common questions

### Q1. Tell me about receiving hard feedback.

**A:** Feedback that reviews were too design-pure and slowed delivery. I kept the bar on money paths but added lighter templates for low-risk endpoints — earned trust from both quality and speed camps.

### Q2. How do you earn trust with a partner team?

**A:** Explicit event contracts, versioning, sample payloads, joint failure drills on Kafka consumers, and no surprise production publishes.

### Q3. When were you vocally self-critical?

**A:** I underestimated poison-message operational load. I said so in the postmortem and funded DLQ tooling — credibility rose more than if I had spun it.


## Kafka case bank (reuse across follow-ups)

Use these concrete cases when the interviewer digs into Kafka after your story.

| Case | What happened | What you owned | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer lag spike | Downstream HSBC/SWIFT validation slowed; lag grew on payment topic | Tuned concurrency, partitioned hot keys, added lag alerts + backpressure | Restored SLA without data loss |
| Poison message / DLQ | Bad SWIFT payload blocked partition processing | Built DLQ + bulk replay tool with account/SWIFT validation | Incident recovery time **−50%** |
| Duplicate events | Retries after broker blip caused duplicate tax/payment side effects | Idempotency keys + dedupe store; exactly-once *effect* design | Zero double-posting in replay drills |
| Schema / contract break | Producer shipped incompatible event | Compatibility checks in CI, consumer defensive parsing, canary topic | Caught before broad blast radius |
| Rebalance storm | Rolling deploy caused frequent rebalances | `max.poll.interval`, sticky assignor, drain-before-shutdown | Stable throughput during deploys |
| Out-of-order cash events | Same account events across partitions | Key by account/cash-line; ordering within key | Correct reconciliation order |
| Analytics pipeline | Merchant reports took hours from batch DB pulls | Real-time Kafka logging → parallel consumers | Report generation **−90%** time (PayPal) |
| Observability gap | Hard to prove “message landed then failed downstream” | Correlation IDs, lag/DLQ dashboards (Splunk/CloudWatch) | Faster RCA, fewer finger-pointing loops |
