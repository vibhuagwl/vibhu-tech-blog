## Principle

Leaders work every day to create a safer, more productive, higher performing, more diverse, and more just work environment. They lead with empathy, have fun at work, and make it easy for others to have fun. Leaders ask themselves: Are my fellow employees growing? Are they empowered? Are they ready for what’s next? Leaders have a vision for and commitment to their employees’ personal success, whether that be at Amazon or elsewhere.

## How SDEs demonstrate this (without HR theater)

- Reduce chronic pages with better tooling
- Grow people deliberately
- Make reviews kind and rigorous
- Design work so weekends are not the plan

## Primary STAR — Sustainable ops via DLQ Replay Tool

**Situation:** Repeated Kafka failure recovery burned engineers and ops — heroic culture, brittle morale.

**Task:** Improve the employee experience by making recovery a safe, taught, shared capability.

**Action:** Built Replay Tool + runbooks; trained the team of 4 and ops; rotated ownership of alerts; emphasized blameless RCA.

**Result:** Recovery **−50%**; fewer heroics; people could sleep when the tool + alerts worked.

## Backup — Mentorship & empowerment

Architecture forums where quieter engineers present; pairing on Kafka consumers; delegating Playwright framework modules with coaching — growth as a first-class deliverable.

## Common questions

### Q1. How have you improved team wellbeing?

**A:** Attacked toil at the source (manual replay), not pizza for night shifts. Measured pages and recovery duration.

### Q2. Tell me about supporting someone through a hard period.

**A:** After a tough incident, I co-authored the RCA with the on-call engineer, credited their mitigation steps publicly, and scheduled a skills session so the next person was ready — dignity + capability.

### Q3. Diversity & inclusion in engineering practice?

**A:** Structured design reviews with written proposals so ideas are not only for the loudest voice in the room; rotate facilitation.


## Kafka case bank (reuse across follow-ups)

Use these concrete cases when the interviewer digs into Kafka after your story.

| Case | What happened | What you owned | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer lag spike | Downstream HSBC/SWIFT validation slowed; lag grew on payment topic | Tuned concurrency, partitioned hot keys, added lag alerts + backpressure | Restored SLA without data loss |
| Poison message / DLQ | Bad SWIFT payload blocked partition processing | Built DLQ + bulk replay tool with account/SWIFT validation | Incident recovery time **−50%** |
| Duplicate events | Retries after broker blip caused duplicate tax/payment side effects | Idempotency keys + dedupe store; exactly-once *effect* design | Zero double-posting in replay drills |
| Schema / contract break | Producer shipped incompatible event | Compatibility checks in CI, consumer defensive parsing, canary topic | Caught before broad blast radius |
| Rebalance storm | Rolling deploy caused frequent rebalances | `max.poll.interval`, sticky assignor, drain-before-shutdown | Stable throughput during deploys |
| Out-of-order cash events | Same account events across partitions | Key by account/cash-line; ordering within key | Correct reconciliation order |
| Analytics pipeline | Merchant reports took hours from batch DB pulls | Real-time Kafka logging → parallel consumers | Report generation **−90%** time (PayPal) |
| Observability gap | Hard to prove “message landed then failed downstream” | Correlation IDs, lag/DLQ dashboards (Splunk/CloudWatch) | Faster RCA, fewer finger-pointing loops |
