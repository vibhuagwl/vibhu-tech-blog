## Principle (what Amazon means)

Leaders start with the customer and work backwards. They work vigorously to earn and keep customer trust. Although leaders pay attention to competitors, they obsess over customers.

**In an SDE interview:** customers can be end-users, internal operators, partner banks, merchants, or downstream services. Show you felt their pain in metrics and UX, not slogans.

## What interviewers listen for

- You named a **specific customer** and their job-to-be-done
- You measured pain before/after (time, errors, trust)
- You said no to elegant tech that did not help the customer
- You followed through after launch (support, defects, ops)

## Primary STAR — Participant onboarding & cash APIs (JPMorgan)

**Q: Tell me about a time you were customer-obsessed.**

**Situation:** Investment banking participants and equity admins were slowed by fragmented onboarding, cash-line imports, and reconciliation. Partner bank integrations (HSBC/SWIFT) made errors expensive and opaque.

**Task:** I owned secure REST APIs for onboarding, operational bank accounts, cash-line imports, payment batches, reconciliation, and SWIFT validation — with the customer outcome of faster, safer onboarding.

**Action:**
1. Started from operator workflows (where they got stuck), not from service boundaries.
2. Designed APIs around onboarding steps and validation feedback they could act on.
3. Added SWIFT/account validation early so failures were caught before cash movement.
4. Partnered with UAT stakeholders so acceptance criteria matched real participant journeys.
5. Used Kafka paths for async payment/tax side effects so the UI was not blocked on slow downstreams.

**Result:** Participant onboarding accelerated by **~35%**. Operators spent less time in manual rework; downstream integrity improved.

**LP close:** Customer Obsession + Deliver Results + Ownership.

## Backup STAR — Merchant reporting via Kafka (PayPal)

**Situation:** Merchants waited on slow batch reports; support load and trust suffered.

**Task:** Deliver near-real-time reporting without breaking payment correctness.

**Action:** Built Kafka pipelines for transaction logging → analytics/reporting consumers; parallelized processing; preserved data integrity with idempotent consumers.

**Result:** Report generation time **−90%**; availability held at **99.9%** on the broader platform work.

## Common questions & model answers

### Q1. Give an example where you sacrificed a preferred design for the customer.

**A:** I preferred a pure async Kafka-only onboarding status model, but operators needed immediate validation errors during cash-line import. I kept synchronous validation for user-facing checks and async Kafka for heavy downstream (tax/payment batch). Customers got fast feedback; systems stayed scalable.

### Q2. How do you gather customer input as a backend engineer?

**A:** Shadow support/UAT tickets, sit in reconciliation war rooms, read failed SWIFT payloads with ops, instrument drop-off in onboarding APIs, and define SLIs that match customer language (time-to-onboard, time-to-reconcile), not only CPU.

### Q3. Tell me about a time a customer was unhappy.

**A:** After a lag incident, ops could not replay failed SWIFT messages safely. I treated ops as the customer: shipped a DLQ Replay Tool with bulk validation so they recovered without engineering heroics. Recovery time **−50%**.

### Q4. How do you balance customer requests vs platform health?

**A:** I separate “customer promise” (correct money movement, clear errors) from “implementation wish.” If a request risks double-posting, I push back with a safer API/event contract and a timeline. Trust > speed that breaks money.

## Kafka case bank (reuse across follow-ups)

Use these concrete cases when the interviewer digs into Kafka after your story.

| Case | What happened | What you owned | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer lag spike | Downstream HSBC/SWIFT validation slowed; lag grew on payment topic | Tuned concurrency, partitioned hot keys, added lag alerts + backpressure | Restored SLA without data loss |
| Poison message / DLQ | Bad SWIFT payload blocked partition processing | Built DLQ + bulk replay tool with account/SWIFT validation | Incident recovery time **−50%** |
| Duplicate events | Retries after broker blip caused duplicate tax/payment side effects | Idempotency keys + dedupe store; exactly-once *effect* design | Zero double-posting in replay drills |
| Schema / contract break | Producer shipped incompatible event | Compatibility checks in CI, consumer defensive parsing, canary topic | Caught before broad blast radius |
| Rebalance storm | Rolling deploy caused frequent rebalances | `max.poll.interval`, sticky assignor, drain-before-shutdown | Stable throughput during deploys |
| Out-of-order cash events | Same account events across partitions | Key by account/cash-line; ordering within key | Correct reconciliation order |
| Analytics pipeline | Merchant reports took hours from batch DB pulls | Real-time Kafka logging → parallel consumers | Report generation **−90%** time (PayPal) |
| Observability gap | Hard to prove “message landed then failed downstream” | Correlation IDs, lag/DLQ dashboards (Splunk/CloudWatch) | Faster RCA, fewer finger-pointing loops |
