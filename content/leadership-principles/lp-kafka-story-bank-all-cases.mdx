## Case 1 — Consumer lag spike

**Prompt:** Tell me about a time a system was missing SLA.

**STAR**
- **S:** Payment/SWIFT consumer lag grew; reconciliation risked missing windows.
- **T:** Restore SLA without losing messages or corrupting cash state.
- **A:** Split broker health vs consumer lag vs downstream validation latency; found slow SWIFT/account validation + hot accounts; tuned concurrency after idempotency; added lag alerts.
- **R:** Lag recovered; later prevented recurrence with SLOs and the replay path for failures.

**Follow-ups**
- *How measured?* `records-lag`, consume vs produce rate, downstream p99.
- *Why not only add partitions?* Hot key / per-account ordering would not improve.
- *Customer?* Ops + participants waiting on cash/reconciliation correctness.

## Case 2 — Poison message blocked a partition

**Prompt:** Tell me about a production defect that kept failing.

**STAR**
- **S:** Bad SWIFT payload threw repeatedly; partition progress stalled.
- **T:** Unblock processing and stop silent data loss.
- **A:** Classified poison vs transient; routed to DLQ with headers/reason; kept good messages flowing; forbade infinite retry storms.
- **R:** Throughput restored; clear quarantine for bad data.

**Follow-ups**
- *At-least-once implications?* Yes — design idempotent side effects.
- *Who fixes payload?* Upstream producer + ops validation; not “drop forever” without audit.

## Case 3 — DLQ Replay Tool (flagship story)

**Prompt:** Ownership / Invent and Simplify / Bias for Action.

**STAR**
- **S:** Recovery was manual, slow, error-prone.
- **T:** Safe bulk replay with account + SWIFT validation.
- **A:** Built Replay Tool, runbooks, metrics (DLQ depth, replay success/fail); trained ops and team of 4.
- **R:** Incident recovery time **−50%**; integrity strengthened.

**Follow-ups**
- *Two-way door?* Guarded manual replay during incident; tool became the standard.
- *Security?* Validation gates; least privilege for replay publish.
- *How do you know it worked?* Replay success metrics + reconciliation checks.

## Case 4 — Duplicate events / exactly-once *effects*

**Prompt:** Are Right A Lot / Highest Standards.

**STAR**
- **S:** Broker blip + retries risked duplicate tax/payment side effects.
- **T:** Prevent double-posting while keeping at-least-once delivery.
- **A:** Idempotency keys, dedupe store / unique constraints, consumer design for safe retries; rejected “just enable EOS and relax app checks” as insufficient across DB + external calls.
- **R:** Zero double-posting in replay drills; confidence under retries.

**Follow-ups**
- *EOS transactions?* Discuss when applicable; still need business idempotency for external systems.
- *What unique key?* Business natural key (payment id / event id), not only Kafka offset.

## Case 5 — Ordering & hot keys

**Prompt:** Dive Deep / Disagree and Commit.

**STAR**
- **S:** Out-of-order cash events broke reconciliation assumptions.
- **T:** Preserve per-account order without killing throughput.
- **A:** Key by account/cash-line; accepted partition hotspot trade-off; optimized slow validation path instead of random keys.
- **R:** Correct order; lag addressed at the real bottleneck.

**Follow-ups**
- *Trade-off?* Ordering vs max parallelism — chose correctness for money.
- *Could you shard accounts?* Yes for extreme hot keys; explain sticky routing carefully.

## Case 6 — Rebalance storm on deploy

**Prompt:** Deliver Results / Dive Deep.

**STAR**
- **S:** Rolling deploys caused frequent rebalances; throughput dipped.
- **T:** Keep processing stable through releases.
- **A:** Tuned `max.poll.interval`, cooperative/sticky assignor, drain-before-shutdown; verified in lower env with deploy drill.
- **R:** Stable throughput during deploys; fewer false lag pages.

## Case 7 — Schema / contract break

**Prompt:** Earn Trust / Disagree and Commit / Think Big.

**STAR**
- **S:** Producer nearly shipped incompatible event to shared topic.
- **T:** Protect all consumers without freezing delivery forever.
- **A:** Challenged loose “publish now” plan; proposed versioned contracts + CI compatibility + canary; committed fully once agreed; shared examples with partner teams.
- **R:** Break caught before broad blast radius; trust in event bus improved.

## Case 8 — Real-time reporting pipeline (PayPal)

**Prompt:** Customer Obsession / Think Big / Deliver Results.

**STAR**
- **S:** Merchants waited on slow batch reports.
- **T:** Near-real-time reporting with integrity.
- **A:** Kafka pipelines for transaction logging → analytics consumers; parallel processing; idempotent writes.
- **R:** Report generation **−90%** time; platform work held **99.9%** availability.

## Case 9 — Observability & RCA

**Prompt:** Dive Deep / Earn Trust / Frugality.

**STAR**
- **S:** Hard to prove “event landed then failed downstream.”
- **T:** Make RCA factual across teams.
- **A:** Correlation IDs, lag/DLQ dashboards (Splunk/CloudWatch), later cost-aware analytics migration (**$1M+/yr** savings) without deleting audit trails needed for money.
- **R:** Faster RCA; less finger-pointing; responsible cost.

## Case 10 — Mentoring Kafka competence

**Prompt:** Hire and Develop / Learn and Be Curious / Earth's Best Employer.

**STAR**
- **S:** Team of 4 needed consistent consumer patterns.
- **T:** Raise the bar so Kafka is not tribal knowledge.
- **A:** Design reviews on keys/retries/DLQ; paired RCAs; delegated framework ownership; runbooks so on-call is humane.
- **R:** Consistent delivery; less heroics; people grew into owners.

## Universal follow-up checklist (memorize)

1. What was the **customer** impact in plain language?
2. What **metric** moved (lag, DLQ depth, p99, recovery time)?
3. What were **2–3 options** and why you rejected the others?
4. How did you ensure **no double money movement**?
5. What **prevention** did you leave behind (alert, test, tool, contract)?
6. What would you **do differently** next time?
7. Which **2 LPs** does this demonstrate? Say them explicitly at the end.

I built a DLQ Replay Tool with bulk account and SWIFT validation, plus lag/DLQ metrics and runbooks. Recovery time dropped about 50%, and we stopped treating Kafka failures as one-off heroics. That story shows Ownership, Dive Deep, and Insist on the Highest Standards.”
