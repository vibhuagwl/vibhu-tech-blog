## Principle

Leaders are obligated to respectfully challenge decisions when they disagree, even when doing so is uncomfortable or exhausting. Leaders have conviction and are tenacious. They do not compromise for the sake of social cohesion. Once a decision is determined, they commit wholly.

## Primary STAR — Standards vs shortcut on event contracts

**Situation:** Pressure to ship a payment/tax integration quickly by publishing loosely typed Kafka events and “fix consumers later.”

**Task:** Protect long-term integrity without becoming the team blocker.

**Action:**
1. Disagreed with data: showed prior incidents where schema drift caused reconciliation pain.
2. Proposed a lighter compromise: versioned JSON schema + compatibility check in CI + idempotent consumers — faster than a full mesh, safer than chaos.
3. Escalated via architecture forum with options and risks, not personalities.
4. Once leadership chose the versioned-contract path, I committed fully: templates, review checklist, mentoring.

**Result:** Delivered integration without normalizing poison-events; team velocity recovered through reusable templates.

## Backup — Disagree on “just scale consumers”

I opposed scaling as the first fix for lag; pushed hot-key and validation RCA. After agreement, I committed to implementing consumer tuning *and* the deep fix.

## Common questions

### Q1. Tell me about disagreeing with your manager.

**A:** Manager favored manual replay indefinitely to save eng time. I disagreed with on-call cost and risk math, proposed a time-boxed tool build. Decision: build Replay Tool. I executed without lingering dissent.

### Q2. When did you commit to a decision you disagreed with?

**A:** Chose SQS for Deloitte tax responses vs pure Kafka everywhere. I preferred one bus, but committed to SQS listeners, error handling, and docs — success metrics mattered more than purism.

### Q3. How do you disagree respectfully?

**A:** Restate their goal, present customer risk, offer options with cost, ask what would change their mind, avoid public shaming.


## Kafka case bank (reuse across follow-ups)

Use these concrete cases when the interviewer digs into Kafka after your story.

| Case | What happened | What you owned | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer lag spike | Downstream HSBC/SWIFT validation slowed; lag grew on payment topic | Tuned concurrency, partitioned hot keys, added lag alerts + backpressure | Restored SLA without data loss |
| Poison message / DLQ | Bad SWIFT payload blocked partition processing | Built DLQ + bulk replay tool with account/SWIFT validation | Incident recovery time **−50%** |
| Duplicate events | Retries after broker blip caused duplicate tax/payment side effects | Idempotency keys + dedupe store; exactly-once *effect* design | Zero double-posting in replay drills |
| Schema / contract break | Producer shipped incompatible event | Compatibility checks in CI, consumer defensive parsing, canary topic | Caught before broad blast radius |
| Rebalance storm | Rolling deploy caused frequent rebalances | `max.poll.interval`, sticky assignor, drain-before-shutdown | Stable throughput during deploys |
| Out-of-order cash events | Same account events across partitions | Key by account/cash-line; ordering within key | Correct reconciliation order |
| Analytics pipeline | Merchant reports took hours from batch DB pulls | Real-time Kafka logging → parallel consumers | Report generation **−90%** time (PayPal) |
| Observability gap | Hard to prove “message landed then failed downstream” | Correlation IDs, lag/DLQ dashboards (Splunk/CloudWatch) | Faster RCA, fewer finger-pointing loops |
