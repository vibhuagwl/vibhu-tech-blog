## Principle

Leaders have relentlessly high standards — many people may think these standards are unreasonably high. Leaders are continually raising the bar and drive their teams to deliver high-quality products, services, and processes. Leaders ensure that defects do not get sent down the line and that problems are fixed so they stay fixed.

## Primary STAR — Validation gates on money movement (JPMorgan)

**Situation:** Payment batches and SWIFT data could fail in subtle ways; a “ship and patch” culture would create reconciliation debt.

**Task:** Keep API and event paths correct under regulatory and banking constraints.

**Action:**
1. SWIFT and account validation in APIs and in DLQ replay (no blind re-injection).
2. Architecture standards via forums/reviews for the team of 4.
3. Expanded automated regression (Playwright UI+API) so defects do not escape.
4. Explicit error handling on Deloitte tax SQS responses.

**Result:** Stronger data integrity; recovery **−50%** when issues still occurred; onboarding quality improved with speed (**+35%**).

## Backup — Security & availability (PayPal)

OAuth2-based security for enterprise compliance; Kafka microservices at **99.9%** availability; performance bar raised with measurable latency/throughput targets.

## Common questions

### Q1. Tell me about a time you refused to lower the bar.

**A:** Push to “just republish DLQ to clear the pager.” I refused without validation — wrong SWIFT data would corrupt cash state. We built validated bulk replay instead.

### Q2. How do you keep standards from becoming bureaucracy?

**A:** Encode standards in templates, CI checks, and example PRs. Fast path for low-risk changes; heavy review for money/event contracts.

### Q3. Describe fixing a class of defects.

**A:** Idempotent consumers + dedupe for duplicate Kafka deliveries — fixed an entire failure class, not one ticket.


## Kafka case bank (reuse across follow-ups)

Use these concrete cases when the interviewer digs into Kafka after your story.

| Case | What happened | What you owned | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer lag spike | Downstream HSBC/SWIFT validation slowed; lag grew on payment topic | Tuned concurrency, partitioned hot keys, added lag alerts + backpressure | Restored SLA without data loss |
| Poison message / DLQ | Bad SWIFT payload blocked partition processing | Built DLQ + bulk replay tool with account/SWIFT validation | Incident recovery time **−50%** |
| Duplicate events | Retries after broker blip caused duplicate tax/payment side effects | Idempotency keys + dedupe store; exactly-once *effect* design | Zero double-posting in replay drills |
| Schema / contract break | Producer shipped incompatible event | Compatibility checks in CI, consumer defensive parsing, canary topic | Caught before broad blast radius |
| Rebalance storm | Rolling deploy caused frequent rebalances | `max.poll.interval`, sticky assignor, drain-before-shutdown | Stable throughput during deploys |
| Out-of-order cash events | Same account events across partitions | Key by account/cash-line; ordering within key | Correct reconciliation order |
| Analytics pipeline | Merchant reports took hours from batch DB pulls | Real-time Kafka logging → parallel consumers | Report generation **−90%** time (PayPal) |
| Observability gap | Hard to prove “message landed then failed downstream” | Correlation IDs, lag/DLQ dashboards (Splunk/CloudWatch) | Faster RCA, fewer finger-pointing loops |
