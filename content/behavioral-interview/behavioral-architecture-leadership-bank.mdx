## Flagship — Complex architecture / transformation (Q31–Q34)

**T1:** Payments/trading systems — event-driven Java/Spring microservices, Kafka, cloud. Incremental strangler; RF=3 money paths; outbox for DB↔events.

## Q35–Q37 — Competing architectures / regret / feedback

Hybrid after criteria review (**D1**). Regret: underestimating poison-message ops load → fixed with DLQ product (**R1**). Changed mind after benchmarks for simpler design.

## Q38–Q39 — Scalability vs simplicity; prevent distributed complexity

Scale the bottleneck you measured (**P1**). Don’t distribute by default — distribute when team/deploy/scale boundaries demand it.

## Q40–Q42 — Bottleneck, SPOF, design for failure

Hot partitions, DB wait time, broker AZ placement. Remove SPOFs with replicas/multi-AZ and client failover. Design for failure: timeouts, idempotency, DLQ, degradations.

## Q43–Q45 — Abstraction, sync/async, when microservices

Abstraction earns its keep via reuse/risk reduction. Sync for user-facing validation; async for tax/payment side effects. Microservices when independent deploy + scale + ownership justify cost.

## Q46–Q47 — Microservices wrong / simplicity over scale

Tiny domains with one team — modular monolith better. Chose simpler path when benchmarks met requirements.

## Q48 — Evaluate architecture success

SLOs, change failure rate, lead time, cost, cognitive load, on-call pages, business KPIs — not diagram beauty.
