## 8. Underperforming engineer

**Approach:** Diagnose root cause first — skills, unclear expectations, workload, communication, motivation, or personal circumstances.

Then: clear expectations + measurable goals. Example: system-design gap → pairing, design reviews, ownership of a smaller component. Regular feedback; measure progress.

If improvement: best outcome. If not after support: work the performance-management process with management.

**Close:** Supportive on the person; uncompromising on the standards.

---

## 9. Mentored someone

Moved engineers from task execution to **technical ownership**.

Instead of prescribing implementation, gave the business problem and asked for an architecture proposal. Reviewed design, challenged assumptions, discussed trade-offs, let them own implementation within boundaries. Grew scope over time.

**Close:** Successful mentoring eventually makes you unnecessary for that person’s decisions.

---

## 10. Influenced without authority

**Situation:** Large architecture initiative affected teams that did not report to me.

**Action:** Built a compelling tech + business case (problems, proposed architecture, benefits, migration, risks, per-team impact). Invited those engineers to challenge the design — creating shared ownership.

**Result:** Adoption through understanding and participation, not hierarchy.

**Close:** Influence comes from credibility, clarity, and collaboration.

## 17. Strong personality on the team

Focus on **behavior and outcomes**, not personality labels. Strong opinions are valuable; the problem is silencing others.

Establish reviews where ideas are challenged with evidence. If behavior blocks discussion, address privately and directly.

**Close:** Channel strong opinions into better decisions — don’t dilute them into silence.

## 21. Build high-performing teams

| Pillar | Practice |
|---|---|
| Clear ownership | Every important system has an owner |
| High standards | Design review, quality, security, ops readiness |
| Psychological safety | Challenge architecture; admit mistakes |
| Growth | Progressively larger ownership |
| Business context | Why the system matters to customers |

**Close:** No single technical hero — multiple people capable of strong independent decisions.

---

## 27. What would your team say about you?

Technically strong, approachable, owns outcomes; challenges assumptions and asks “why”; high standards with support to meet them; engineers become more capable and independent over time.
