## Mentoring flagship (Q129–Q132, Q137)

**M1:** Move engineers from tickets → owning designs. They propose architecture; you challenge trade-offs; they ship. Grow scope. Success = you become unnecessary for their decisions.

## Q133–Q136 — Future leaders, succession, knowledge

Signals: judgment under ambiguity, teaching others, written clarity, ownership beyond tickets. Succession via paired ownership, architecture forums, runbooks. Kill silos with docs, rotations, shared on-call.

## Q138–Q143 — Feedback & performance

Refuse feedback → private, specific examples, business impact, expectations. Difficult feedback: behavior+outcome, not identity. Not well received → stay calm, ask understanding, follow up in writing. High performers: public credit + harder problems. Average→strong: deliberate practice on design. Disruptive strong tech: channel via standards; coach behavior; escalate if needed.

## Q144–Q155 — Hiring & org design

Hire for: problem framing, trade-offs, collaboration, production ownership — not puzzle speed alone. Architects: systems thinking + influence. Strong engineer ≠ only strong coder — includes operability and judgment. Leadership potential: multiplies others. Best hire: raised team bar. Worst hire: lesson on signal quality. Principal: technical depth + org leverage. Balance specialists/generalists by domain criticality. Team by business capability; align to services. Large distributed systems: clear interfaces, platform vs product teams. Prevent bottlenecks: self-service platforms, contracts, not ticket gatekeeping.
